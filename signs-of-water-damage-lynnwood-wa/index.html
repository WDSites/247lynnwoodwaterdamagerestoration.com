Signs of Water Damage in Lynnwood, WA Homes

Most water damage shows up in small ways for weeks before it becomes a big repair. This is what to look for, where to look, and when to call.

The signs of water damage in Lynnwood, WA homes are often quiet. A faint ceiling ring, a floor that feels slightly soft, or a smell in a closet can be the only hint that water has been moving through a wall for weeks.

Catching it early keeps a drying job small. Below you will find the visual signs, the less obvious clues, a symptom-to-cause table, and a checklist you can run through once a month.

What you can see: stains, paint, and wallpaper

Brown or yellow rings on a ceiling are the classic sign. They grow outward in rings as the area dries and gets wet again, so a stain with several edges tells you water has returned more than once.

Paint that bubbles, peels, or looks blistered is another sign, because moisture behind it pushes the film off. Drywall that looks slightly wavy, has dark seams, or feels cool and damp to the hand is holding water. Press gently near a baseboard. Soft, crumbly drywall is already damaged.

What you can smell, hear, and feel

A musty or earthy smell in a room that has no obvious source is worth chasing. Mold needs moisture to grow, so a persistent odor often means a damp spot somewhere in the wall, floor, or crawl space. A smell alone does not tell you what is growing, and any health question belongs with a physician.

Listen for a faint hiss or a drip inside a wall when every tap is off. Feel the floor too, since a section that is warmer, cooler, or softer than the rest may be sitting on wet subfloor.

Floors that warp, cup, or lift

Hardwood that cups, with the edges higher than the centers, has absorbed moisture from below. Boards that buckle and lift off the subfloor have taken on more. Laminate that swells at the seams or feels spongy under foot has water in its core.

Tile floors give clues too. Cracked grout, loose tiles, or a hollow sound when you tap one can mean water has loosened the bed under it. Do not wait for the whole floor to move, because the first few boards are the warning.

Where to look in older homes

Older houses hide water in predictable places. Check the cabinet under each sink, the area behind and under the toilet, around the washing machine hoses, and beneath the water heater. Look at the ceiling below any bathroom and along the walls under windows.

Attics and crawl spaces matter too. Shine a light at roof penetrations, around vents, and on plumbing runs for stains or dripping. If you find standing water in a crawl space, treat it as active and call for help. Stay away from wet wiring and any electrical panel that is damp.

Your water bill as a clue, not a diagnosis

A bill that jumps with no change in habits can hint at a hidden leak, such as a running toilet or a line failing underground. To check, read your meter, turn off every fixture for a short time, and see whether the dial still moves.

A high bill alone does not prove damage, and a normal bill does not rule it out, since a slow leak into a wall may be small. Use the bill to prompt a closer look, not as the only test.

A monthly checklist

  • Look under every sink for dampness, rust on fittings, or warped cabinet floors.
  • Check ceilings and the upper corners of walls for new stains or paint changes.
  • Inspect the washing machine hoses and the water heater base.
  • Press on floors near tubs, toilets, and dishwashers for soft spots.
  • Scan attic and crawl space for stains or drips after heavy rain.
  • Note any new musty smell and where it is strongest.

Write the date you spotted a sign and photograph it. Small records help later if the damage grows.

Questions we hear

Which utility do I contact about my water meter in Lynnwood?

The City of Lynnwood's utility bill covers water and sewer, but its 2012 water plan notes some addresses inside the city are billed directly by the Alderwood Water & Wastewater District. That plan may be dated.

Look at your bill. The name at the top tells you who to call about the meter and any unusual usage.

Can I tell if mold is growing by looking at a stain?

No. A stain only shows moisture has been there. Only testing by a qualified professional tells you what is growing, and remediation follows the ANSI/IICRC S520 standard.

Fix the leak first, then dry the area. Take health questions to a physician.

A ceiling stain looks old. Do I still need to act?

Yes, at least to check it. An old stain may be dry, or it may be wet every time it rains or someone showers.

Press it, look above it, and photograph it. A moisture meter reading tells you if it is active.

My floor feels fine, but there is a musty smell. What now?

The water may be inside a wall or under the floor, where you cannot see it. Look near plumbing walls, exterior walls, and low corners.

Call the 24/7 line if the smell persists. Crews use moisture meters and thermal cameras to find hidden wet spots.
