First priority: flood damage restoration for the river and the sloughs

In December 2025 the city monitored Snohomish River flooding, warned that Riverfront Blvd might close, sandbagged the Water Pollution Control Facility and closed Rotary Park, Langus Riverfront Park and Lowell-River Road at Rotary Park. That is the kind of event that sends water into North Everett garages and lower floors.

A Snohomish County fact sheet says the Ebey Slough, Steamboat and Union Slough, Ebey Island and Marshland levees are non-accredited, and that homeowner insurance usually does not cover flood damage. The Marshlands Flood Control District formed in 1960 after a flood covered over 8,000 acres. Second: emergency water removal in the combined sewer area

The city's North End Sewer System is a combined sewer that carries wastewater and stormwater together, and the city is separating older north-end pipes to reduce basement flooding. During the December 2025 event, the city told owners with backwater valves to wait 10 to 15 minutes after heavy rain before using fixtures. That advice shows how fast a basement can take on a backup, and emergency water removal is the first response.

Surface water plans also list inundation projects at Evergreen Way and Madison Street for Pigeon Creek No. 1 and at Dull Place in the Bay Bluffs basin. Runoff can collect at low streets in those areas, so garages and lower floors there deserve quick pumping.

Third: water mitigation for homes from the 1940s to the 1970s

Everett's housing element says about half of the city's housing units were built before 1980, with over 7,200 before 1940. The Delta neighborhood has houses from 1920 to 1970, and Boulevard Bluffs from 1940 to 1980. Older framing, finishes and supply lines can hold moisture differently than new construction, so water mitigation relies on moisture meters, thermal imaging and LGR dehumidifiers to find what the surface hides.

Only 49 percent of units are owner-occupied, and about 17 percent are in buildings of 20 or more units. Tenants should call the landlord or manager quickly, and then call us. In larger buildings one leak often touches several units.

Fourth: cleanup when the water is dirty

The city runs about 334 miles of sewer main and 33 lift stations, and its surface water program covers 23 drainage basins that flow to Lake Washington, the Snohomish River and Port Gardner Bay. When a backup or flood carries sewage, water damage cleanup covers removal of porous materials, sanitizing and drying. Last: mold, rebuild and permits

Materials that stay wet turn into mold problems. Mold remediation follows the ANSI/IICRC S520 standard and begins only after the moisture source is fixed. Rebuilding may need a permit, and Permit Services at 2930 Wetmore Ave handles applications through an online portal.

Panel, gas and sewage: do not wade in

If water has reached the electrical panel or outlets, or you smell gas, get out and call 911 or your electric or gas utility before calling us. Do not enter a flooded basement with the power on. Sewage and river water are contaminated, so keep children and pets out.

Before we arrive: four steps while you wait

Stop what you can

Close the main valve or fixture shutoff. If river or sewer water is rising from outside or a drain, no valve helps, so skip ahead.

Power, only if dry

Turn off breakers to wet rooms only if the panel is dry and you can reach it on dry floor. Otherwise call the utility and wait.

Get things off the floor

Lift electronics, files and keepsakes onto upper shelves. Move rugs and small furniture to dry rooms.

Stay out of dirty water

Keep family and pets away from river, storm or sewage water. Then call the 24/7 emergency line.
