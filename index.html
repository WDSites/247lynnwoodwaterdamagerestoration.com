Water categories

Water Category Decides How the Job Is Handled

Not all water is equal. The published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard sorts water into three categories by how contaminated it is, and the category decides what can be dried in place and what has to be thrown out.

Water categories at a glance
CategoryWhat it meansWhat is done
Category 1Clean source, such as a broken supply line or an overflowing sink running clean waterWet materials are extracted and dried in place. Carpet and pad can often be saved if the water is caught early.
Category 2Used water, such as a washing machine drain or dishwasher dischargePorous materials like carpet pad are usually removed. Surfaces are cleaned and sanitized before the drying equipment goes in.
Category 3Grossly contaminated water, such as sewage backup or rising surface waterContainment goes up, porous materials come out and surfaces are disinfected. Everyone stays out of the area until the crew says otherwise.

Price follows the same few factors on every job. They are the water category, the size of the affected area, the materials involved, the number of drying days and the amount of equipment, and finally the rebuild scope. A clean supply-line leak in one bathroom and a sewage backup across a finished basement sit at opposite ends.

Insurance

Documentation That Helps Your Insurance Claim

Sudden and accidental water damage, like a burst pipe, is often covered by a homeowner policy. Gradual leaks and long-term neglect often are not, and flood damage is usually excluded unless you carry separate flood coverage. Check your policy and ask your insurer what applies to your loss.

What we can do is document the loss clearly. Photos of the damage, moisture readings by room and a written scope of work give you organized material for your own claim.

Keep your own records too

Photograph the source and every affected room before anything is moved. Keep receipts for emergency purchases, and save damaged items until your insurer says they can go.

First minutes

What to Do in the First Minutes of a Water Emergency

What you do before a crew arrives changes the outcome. A fast, calm response can save flooring and drywall that would otherwise be lost.

  1. Stop the source. Shut the fixture valve or, if you cannot find it, the main water valve.
  2. Cut power to wet areas only if the electrical panel is dry and you can reach it safely.
  3. Lift small furniture, rugs and valuables off wet floors, and pull open cabinet doors under sinks.
  4. Photograph the source, the water and every damaged item before moving anything.
  5. Keep children and pets out of the water, especially if it came from a drain or toilet.
  6. Call the 24/7 line and describe what you see so the crew can plan equipment.

Stay out if electricity, gas or sewage is involved

If water is touching outlets, the electrical panel or an appliance, do not step in it. If you smell gas, leave the house. Call your electric or gas utility, or 911 for any electrical or gas risk, before you call the restoration line.

Sewage and toilet backup water is contaminated. Keep everyone out of it and wait for a crew with the right gear.

Water damage questions in Lynnwood

Can someone just come look at the damage?

Yes. An inspection is a normal first step, and the crew checks moisture levels, finds where the water went and explains what the job would involve. Call the line and describe the source, the rooms and how long the water has been there.

We cannot promise an arrival time, but we offer same-day response when a crew is available.

How much notice do you need to schedule an inspection?

There is no long lead time. Call as soon as you notice a problem, whatever the hour, because every day a wet material stays wet makes the repair larger.

If the water is still running, shut off the main valve first, then call.

How do you explain pricing and scope before work starts?

After the inspection, the crew walks you through what will be done in each room, what drives the price and what could change it once walls are opened. You decide before equipment goes in.

Price depends on water category, affected area, materials, drying days and equipment, and the rebuild scope. For a real number on your house, call the 24/7 line.

What documentation helps with an insurance claim?

Photos of the source and each affected room, moisture readings, a written scope of work and receipts for emergency costs are the core items. Our crews record photos, readings and a scope so you have organized material for your own claim.

Ask your insurer what your policy covers and what it needs from you.

Is water damage restoration the same as basement waterproofing?

No. Restoration removes water that has already entered and dries and repairs what got wet. Waterproofing is a different trade that changes drainage, grading or foundation details to keep water out in the future.

If your basement keeps taking on water, you likely need both, and we will tell you plainly when the cause calls for a waterproofing or drainage specialist.

What happens when a whole floor is affected?

A loss that size is handled as a larger project. The crew extracts the water, removes materials that cannot be dried, sets many air movers and several LGR dehumidifiers, and checks readings daily.

Expect more drying days and a bigger rebuild scope. The scope and price are explained before the work starts.

How long does drying take?

Most structural drying runs about three to five days, depending on the materials, the water category and how long the water sat. Thick framing and hardwood take longer than drywall.

The crew checks moisture readings each visit and removes equipment only when the materials read dry against the standard.

Do you handle water emergencies at night or on weekends in Lynnwood?

Yes. The 24/7 line is open at night, on weekends and on holidays, and same-day response is offered when a crew is available. We do not promise a specific arrival time.

While you wait, shut off the water, keep clear of wet electrical areas and lift valuables off the floor.
