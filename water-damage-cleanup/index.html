What cleanup covers once the water source is stopped

Cleanup is the hands-on removal stage. Crews pull out standing water, wet debris, ruined flooring layers and anything the water contaminated, then clean and sanitize the surfaces that remain. It comes after the leak or flood source is stopped and before the drying stage begins.

Drying is its own job with its own gear. If walls and subfloor are still wet after the debris is gone, read how structural drying works and why it usually takes several days.

The goal is plain. Take out what cannot be saved, protect what can, and leave clean surfaces ready for drying and rebuild.

The first 48 hours decide how much comes out

Wet drywall, pad and insulation break down fast. Baseboards swell and paint bubbles within a day. By the second day, damp materials often smell musty, and hidden cavities behind cabinets stay wet even when the room feels dry to the touch.

This is the usual sequence on a cleanup job, from the first call to the handoff to drying.

  1. First hours: stop the source, shut the main valve, move valuables and photograph the damage.
  2. Day one: extract standing water, pull soaked carpet pad and bag wet debris.
  3. Day two: cut out materials that cannot dry, clean the exposed surfaces and apply antimicrobial treatment.
  4. Day three onward: air movers and LGR dehumidifiers dry the structure while crews take moisture meter readings at each visit.

What gets removed and what gets saved

Crews sort materials one by one with a moisture meter and a thermal imaging camera, not by guesswork. The same material often gets a different answer depending on the water category.

CarpetOften lifted, dried and saved if caught earlyUsually discarded

Framing and concrete almost always stay. They get cleaned and dried instead of torn out, which keeps the rebuild smaller.

Category 2 and Category 3 water handling

Category 1 water comes from a clean source, like a broken supply line. Category 2 carries some contamination, such as washing machine or dishwasher overflow. Category 3 is the worst grade and includes sewage, toilet backup with waste and water that came in from outside.

Water also moves up a category as it sits, so a day-old leak can be treated more strictly than a fresh one. Porous materials soaked by Category 3 water, like carpet, pad and drywall, come out rather than getting cleaned in place.

Crews wear protective gear, seal off the work area with plastic sheeting and bag contaminated debris on site. Hard surfaces and framing get a cleaning pass, then antimicrobial treatment.

Sewage, floodwater or electrical risk: stay out first

If water has reached outlets, the electrical panel or a gas appliance, stay out of the room. Call your electric utility (Snohomish County PUD per the city's public works page), your gas utility (Puget Sound Energy) or 911 before you call the restoration line.

If sewage is involved, keep everyone out of the area, including pets, and do not wade in to rescue items. Call us from a dry room.

Antimicrobial treatment and deodorizing

After the debris is out, crews wipe or spray exposed framing, subfloor and concrete with an antimicrobial product matched to the surface. Fine dust from demolition is picked up with a HEPA vacuum, and HEPA air scrubbers run while the work is under way.

Odor leaves when its source leaves. Spraying fragrance over a wet pad only hides the smell for a day or two, so deodorizing comes after removal. Crews then use air scrubbing or thermal fogging where the smell has soaked into the structure.

What drives the cost of cleanup

Cleanup pricing follows scope, not a flat rate. These are the factors that move it most:

  • Water category, since Category 3 needs more removal, protective gear and disposal.
  • Affected area, meaning how many rooms and how much flooring, wall and ceiling is wet.
  • Materials involved, because carpet and pad come out faster than tile, plaster or hardwood.
  • Access and debris volume, including stairs, crawl spaces and haul-out distance.
  • The drying stage that follows, and the rebuild scope after that.

Scope and price are explained before work starts. For a real number on your house, call the 24/7 line.

Insurance and cleanup records

A general note on insurance

Sudden and accidental water damage, such as a burst pipe, is often covered, while gradual leaks and neglect often are not. Coverage differs by policy, so check your policy or ask your insurer before you decide anything.

Crews photograph the damage, take moisture readings and write down what was removed, which gives you material for your own claim. Keep any receipts and bag samples of removed materials if your insurer asks for them.

Questions about this service

Do you handle water damage cleanup emergencies at night or on weekends in Lynnwood?

Yes. The line is answered at all hours, and we offer same-day response when available, including overnight and on weekends and holidays. A burst pipe or sewage backup does not wait for business hours, and neither does the cleanup.

While you wait for the crew, shut the main water valve, move valuables off wet floors and keep people out of any water that came from a drain.

What is the difference between water damage cleanup and drying?

Cleanup removes water, wet debris and contaminated materials, then sanitizes and deodorizes. Drying uses air movers and LGR dehumidifiers to pull moisture out of the materials that stay.

Most jobs need both, usually one right after the other. Cleanup comes first so that drying equipment is not working against materials that cannot be saved.

My carpet is soaked after a leak. Can it be saved?

Often yes, if the water was clean, it has not sat long and the pad is replaced. The carpet is lifted, dried and put back, while the pad is usually thrown away.

If the water came from a sewer, a toilet with waste or outside flooding, carpet is usually discarded. Crews confirm with a moisture meter before deciding.

What are Category 2 and Category 3 water, and why do they matter?

The categories describe how contaminated the water is. Category 1 is clean supply water, Category 2 has some contamination, and Category 3 includes sewage and floodwater from outside.

Higher categories mean more material removed, more protective gear and stricter cleaning. They are part of the published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, and you can ask any company whether its crews work to it.

Does cleanup include sanitizing and odor removal?

Yes. After debris is removed, surfaces get an antimicrobial treatment, and HEPA air scrubbers run during demolition. Deodorizing follows removal, because smell comes from the wet material itself.

If a musty smell remains after drying, tell the crew. It can point to a damp cavity that has not been opened yet.

Will my insurance pay for water damage cleanup?

Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, and gradual leaks often are not. Flood damage is usually excluded from standard homeowners policies and needs separate flood coverage.

Check your policy or ask your insurer. Crews document the loss with photos and moisture readings so you have records for your own claim.

Can I clean up water damage myself?

Small clean-water spills on hard floors, yes. Anything involving sewage, outside floodwater, wet insulation or water inside walls is better left to a crew with extraction units, protective gear and moisture meters.

Never touch wet outlets or stand in water near the panel. Call your electric utility or 911 first if there is any electrical risk.
