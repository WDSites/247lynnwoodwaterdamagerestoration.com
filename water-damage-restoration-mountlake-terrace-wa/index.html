Post-war houses: what a 1950s or 1960s home hides

The city's 2017 housing element says about half of Mountlake Terrace homes were built before 1970 and nearly 86 percent before 1990, with only about 6 percent after 2000. The land use element adds that the city began with small houses for returning veterans, followed by subdivisions on lots of 7,500 to 10,000 square feet. Homes that old often have original supply lines and drains, so an emergency here may be a tired fitting rather than a storm.

Single Household 7200 zoning covers about 644 of the city's 2,039 acres, so single-family blocks anchor much of what we plan for. We check crawl spaces and subfloors with a moisture meter, since water travels along joists before it shows.

Town Center, Town Square and the apartments

About 37 percent of the city's housing is multifamily, and in those buildings damage often starts one floor up. An overflowing fixture wets the ceiling, wall cavities and light fixtures below, so drying must follow the water through every floor it touched.

Melody Hill, the steep flanks and the high ground

The city's long-range plan says Mountlake Terrace holds the highest elevation in southwest Snohomish County, from 263 to 528 feet, and calls the steep east and west flanks unstable for development. Melody Hill, a 350-acre area west of I-5 along the 220th St SW corridor, borders Edmonds and Lynnwood. Surface water runs downhill, so garages and basements at the foot of those slopes take the heavy flow during a long rain.

Lake Ballinger, Hall Creek and Lyon Creek

Lake Ballinger covers 101 acres, and 66 of them lie inside Mountlake Terrace. The plan calls stormwater from upstream buildings, parking lots and roads a major problem for the Hall Creek and Lake Ballinger system, and notes that the city removed a Hall Creek culvert in 2011. Homes near the shore or creek banks may see high groundwater after long rain, so a damp basement wall deserves a moisture reading.

FEMA has mapped a 100-year flood boundary in the city, so check the FEMA flood map for your address. Flood damage is usually excluded from standard homeowner policies. A Lyon Creek detention facility dates to 1985, yet no basin stops a plumbing failure indoors.

When water is spreading in Mountlake Terrace

Panel wet, gas smell or sewage? Hold off and call out first

When water reaches the electrical panel, outlets or a gas line, step outside and call 911 or your electric or gas utility before you call a restoration crew. Sewage means bacteria, so keep everyone out of the room until crews arrive in protective gear.

Before we arrive: what to do in the first few minutes

Shut off the supply

Find the main valve, usually where the line enters the garage or crawl space, and close it fully. Run one tap to see whether the pipes empty.

Dry panel, then breakers

Flip breakers for soaked rooms only when you are standing on dry floor and the panel is clear of water. Otherwise wait and let the utility decide.

Get belongings up

Stack books, devices, photos and cushions on high, dry surfaces.

Phone last, but promptly

Move children and pets away from sewage or standing water, then call the 24/7 line and describe the source, the rooms involved and the color of the water.

One city utility for water, sewer and storm

The city says it provides water, sewer and stormwater utilities within its limits, so a main-line problem has one place to call, including after hours. Its sewer system has 69 miles of main and three lift stations, and about 80 percent of flow goes to the Edmonds plant. The other 20 percent passes through Brier and Lake Forest Park to King County's Brightwater plant.

A backup in the street main is usually the city's to fix, while a clogged lateral on your property is usually yours, so a quick call to the city sorts out who does what. The Edmonds plant also serves Edmonds to the west, and we cover that city too.

Services, starting with drying

In older houses the main job is usually water mitigation: air movers, LGR dehumidifiers and moisture meters running for days until framing reads dry. Ask any company whether its crews work to the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard.

If a leak was slow, check for mold. Mold remediation needs the moisture fixed first, follows the ANSI/IICRC S520 standard and leaves health questions to your physician. For a sudden flood, emergency water removal pulls standing water out first. We also serve Bothell to the southeast.

Questions we hear

Do you cover every ZIP code in Mountlake Terrace?

Yes. The city has one ZIP, 98043, and we cover all of it. That single ZIP wraps Town Center east of I-5, Melody Hill and the 220th St SW corridor to the west, the Gateway area and the Lake Ballinger shoreline, and the directory lists Mountlake Ter as an alias.

Is emergency water removal available in Mountlake Terrace?

Yes. Emergency dispatch is available around the clock, including nights, weekends and holidays. Call the 24/7 line and tell the dispatcher which side of I-5 you are on.

My house was built in the 1950s. What should I check after a small leak?

Look at the cabinet floor, the wall behind it and any ceiling below. Older homes hide moisture in subfloor and insulation, so readings with a moisture meter beat guessing.

A neighbor's leak above me is dripping through my ceiling. What now?

Move belongings, put a bucket under the drip, and ask the owner or building manager to shut off the water upstairs. Then call our 24/7 line, because the wet drywall and insulation need to be opened and dried.

Who handles a sewer backup, the city or me?

If the problem is in the street main, the city's utility handles it. If it is in your own lateral, it is generally yours. Call the city first to find out which, then call us to clean and dry what the backup touched.
