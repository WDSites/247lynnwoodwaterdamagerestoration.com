The wet season: when Mukilteo's ground stops absorbing

The city's 2024 to 2030 surface water plan says most uplands are unsuitable for shallow infiltration because of glacial till. Rain that cannot sink in runs toward about 4,800 city-owned catch basins, and the plan counts roughly 124 acres of wetlands, mostly on the southern plateau. After days of steady rain, expect water at garage thresholds, window wells and the low side of slabs.

Bluff and gulch runoff

Stormwater in Mukilteo drains to 14 watersheds, six of them entirely inside the city, and about 20 miles of stream cut through deep ravines in landslide-prone bluffs. The plan names Big Gulch, Japanese Gulch, Smuggler's Gulch, Goat Trail and Picnic Point among the creeks, and residents have raised local flooding concerns in the Smuggler's Gulch basin. A lot that backs onto a ravine can see a plugged culvert or soaked slope push water toward the foundation.

Public Works helps with landslide mitigation after heavy rain saturates the ground, and the city flags Picnic Point Ravine and the Big Gulch areas as high-priority stormwater retrofit sites. If soil is moving toward your home, call the city first, then call us for emergency water removal of whatever got inside.

Ferry and shoreline weather

The city's floodplain page lists waterfront areas, Naketa Beach and portions of the BNSF railway as flood-prone and cites FEMA map panel 53061C1010F for the waterfront. A property in the floodplain with a federally regulated or insured lender must carry flood insurance. Salt water pushed ashore is a flood event, so flood damage restoration applies, and a standard homeowner policy often excludes it.

The newer ferry terminal opened in December 2020, about one-third mile east of the old 1950s one, and SR 525 carries ferry traffic through town. Wind-driven rain on an exposed shoreline can push moisture behind siding, window trim and roof flashing, where it stays hidden. Ask crews to take readings on exterior walls after any big blow.

When water is spreading in Mukilteo

A flooded garage, a ceiling stain that is growing or sewage in a drain is an emergency, and you can reach the 24/7 line at any hour. Give the dispatcher your cross street and say whether water is coming from above, below or outside. Emergency dispatch is available around the clock, including stormy nights.

Live power, gas or sewage: step back and call first

Water near the electrical panel, wet outlets or any gas appliance calls for 911 or your electric or gas utility before you call us. Stay out of a flooded basement if the power is on. Sewage is its own hazard, so keep children and pets out and wait for a crew with protective equipment.

Before we arrive: four quick steps

Stop it at the source

Close the fixture shutoff or the main valve. If the water is coming from outside, block the door gap with towels or sandbags if you have them, and stay clear of slopes.

Power off only if dry

Turn off breakers for wet areas only if the panel is dry and you do not have to cross water to reach it. When in doubt, leave it and phone the utility.

Move the valuables

Take electronics, documents and keepsakes to an upper floor or a high shelf.

Clear the area, then dial

Keep people and pets away from muddy or sewage-laced water. Then call the 24/7 line and describe the water and the rooms involved.

Old Town, Midtown and Harbour Pointe: three ages of housing

Old Town was incorporated in 1947, Midtown was annexed in 1980 and Harbour Pointe, a master-planned community, in 1991. One city therefore holds mid-century construction, 1980s framing and newer homes with different drain layouts. About 65 percent of units have three or more bedrooms and about 31 percent of households rent, so a laundry line or an upstairs unit is a common source.

Mukilteo Speedway forms a boundary for Harbour Pointe, so give the dispatcher that road name when your address is hard to find.

The Mukilteo Water and Wastewater District posts its own construction notices on the city website, and Everett's annual report lists the district as a wholesale water customer. Alderwood Water and Wastewater District holds a franchise agreement with Mukilteo, but public pages do not say which streets each provider serves, so check your bill before calling about a main.

Repairs after a loss may need a permit from the Permit Center in Planning and Community Development. Our crews also work in Everett to the north and Edmonds down the shoreline.

Services, matched to the season

In a storm, water damage cleanup covers muddy or contaminated water, while extraction units and LGR dehumidifiers handle the rest. Ask any company whether its crews work to the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration. After a wet winter, ask about mold remediation, which follows the ANSI/IICRC S520 standard and needs the moisture fixed first.

Cost factors and insurance

A Mukilteo estimate is shaped by what got wet and how far water traveled, including whether it came through a roof, a window or a foundation wall in a rain event. Water category, affected area, finishes, equipment days and rebuild scope make up the price. Ring the 24/7 line to work out a number for your own house.

Insurance tends to cover a sudden break more readily than a slow drip, and groundwater or storm flooding is generally excluded unless you carry flood coverage. Read your policy and ask your insurer what applies. The crew's moisture readings, photographs and written scope are the documents most claims need.

Is emergency water removal available in Mukilteo?

My lot backs onto a ravine and the crawl space is wet every winter. Is that restoration or drainage work?

Both may be needed. We remove standing water and dry the crawl space, and a drainage contractor handles the grading or perimeter drain that keeps it from returning. We do not do waterproofing.

Do I need flood insurance in Mukilteo?

Check the FEMA flood map for your address. The city's floodplain page says properties in the floodplain with a federally regulated or insured lender must carry flood insurance. Ask your insurer what your policy covers.

Can wind-driven rain cause damage I cannot see?

Yes. Water can sit behind siding or trim for weeks. A moisture meter and a thermal imaging camera show wet wall sections without tearing everything open.
